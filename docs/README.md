## The key findings so far

1. **"Cutting out the broker" doesn't mean selling without a license.** In every state, anyone who sells, solicits or negotiates insurance needs a producer license (NAIC Producer Licensing Model Act). An app that quotes and binds policies *is* an agency in legal terms. The realistic aim is to **replace the broker's manual work and cost with software**, not to avoid licensing.
2. **The broker's cut is built into the price.** Personal auto and home commissions average about 14% of premium. Commercial runs higher. That is the margin a direct model can compete on.
3. **Going direct isn't automatically cheaper.** GEICO's expense ratio is about 10–13% and Progressive's is about 25%, but direct writers replace commissions with large advertising budgets. Customer acquisition cost is the main business risk.
4. **Personal lines already sell direct; commercial lines don't.** Independent agents place about 39% of personal-lines premium but about 87% of commercial-lines premium. Small commercial is the larger gap, and NEXT Insurance's $2.6B sale to ERGO (Munich Re) shows the market values it.
5. **Claims are the pain point people feel most.** Home claims in 2025 averaged 44 days from first notice to final payment, the longest J.D. Power has recorded. Satisfaction drops sharply after 10 days.
