# 04 — The Brokerage Process, Start to Finish

The process an independent agent or retail broker follows. Personal lines (home, auto) is the short version. Commercial lines run longer and use more paperwork.

| # | Stage | What the broker does | Time / effort | Can software replace it? |
|---|-------|----------------------|---------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | **Prospecting** | Referrals, leads bought from EverQuote and similar, local marketing, cross-selling | High cost per lead | Yes, through marketing and SEO (but the cost moves to ads) |
| 2 | **Discovery / risk profile** | Interviews the client about assets, drivers, property details, business operations, current coverage, loss history | 30–90 min personal; hours to days commercial | **Yes.** Guided forms plus data prefill (property records, vehicle VIN, motor vehicle reports, business data) |
| 3 | **Application / submission** | Fills out ACORD forms (commercial), enters data into carrier portals, or sends a submission to a wholesaler or MGA for hard risks | Repeated data entry into each carrier's portal | **Yes.** Carrier APIs and comparative raters |
| 4 | **Marketing the risk** | Shops it to the carriers they're appointed with (usually 5–15+) and collects quotes | Days to weeks for commercial | **Mostly.** Real-time rating APIs for simple risks; hard risks still need people |
| 5 | **Proposal & advice** | Compares coverage, limits, deductibles and exclusions, and recommends | The broker's real expertise | **Partly.** Plain-language comparison plus licensed humans on call |
| 6 | **Bind** | Client accepts; the broker binds coverage (if authorized), issues a binder or certificate, collects or arranges payment | Minutes to a day | **Yes** |
| 7 | **Policy issuance & delivery** | Checks the carrier's policy against what was quoted | Error-prone | **Yes.** Automated document checks |
| 8 | **Servicing** | Endorsements (add a car or driver), certificates of insurance, billing problems, mid-term changes | Ongoing workload | **Yes.** Self-service |
| 9 | **Claims advocacy** | See [03](03-claims-process.md) | Spikes after catastrophes | Partly; needs human escalation |
| 10 | **Renewal / remarketing** | Reviews renewal increases and re-shops if needed | Yearly | **Yes.** Automatic re-shopping is a strong feature |

## How brokers get paid
- **Commission:** a percentage of premium, paid by the carrier and built into the customer's price.
  - Personal auto: about 14% on average (range 6–25%). Homeowners: about 14% on average (range 4.5–22.5%). Source: 2025 benchmark cited by [Sonant](https://www.sonant.ai/blog/insurance-agent-commission-rates-by-line).
  - Carrier example: The Hartford pays 7–16% on auto and 7–15% on home ([schedule](https://www.thehartford.com/personal-lines-producer-compensation)).
  - Captive agents earn less, often 5–10%. Renewals often pay the same or slightly less than new business.
- **Contingent / profit-sharing commissions:** carrier bonuses for volume or low losses. These raise conflict-of-interest questions.
- **Broker fees:** common in commercial and E&S lines; regulated or banned for some personal lines in some states.
- The agency usually splits commission with its individual producer (for example 50/50).

## Requirements to operate as a broker
- **Producer license** in each state where you sell. Applies to both the agency and each individual. Requires a pre-licensing course, a state exam, and ongoing continuing education. Most states issue non-resident licenses through NIPR.
- **Carrier appointments:** each carrier must formally appoint you before you can sell its products. New agencies struggle to get appointments without proven volume, which is why aggregators and agency networks such as SIAA and Smart Choice exist.
- **E&O insurance** (errors & omissions coverage for the agency itself), a **trust account** for premiums in many states, and record-keeping rules.

## Where brokers struggle, and where we have an opening
1. Retyping the same data into each carrier's portal: slow and expensive.
2. Small accounts (under about $1–2k in premium) are barely profitable for a human broker, so they get poor service.
3. Little proactive re-shopping at renewal.
4. Being unreachable at claim time, especially after catastrophes.
5. Conflicts of interest: contingent commissions can push brokers toward certain carriers.
