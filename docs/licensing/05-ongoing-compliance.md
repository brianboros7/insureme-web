# 05 — Ongoing compliance once licensed

## Keep the licenses alive
- **Renewals:** individual and entity licenses in all four states, mostly every 2 years on different dates (Illinois entities expire **May 31** in alternate years). Use NIPR's renewal tools and one compliance calendar.
- **Continuing education:** each licensed person, every renewal cycle (Virginia: 16 hours every 2 years, including 2 of ethics and law). Home-state CE usually counts for non-resident states.
- **Report changes within the state deadline** (often 30 days): new address, new officers or owners, a DRLP change, name or DBA change, and any **administrative action or criminal charge**.
- **Appointments and terminations:** carriers file and terminate appointments; keep our roster in sync.

## Selling rules
- **Anti-rebating:** most states ban giving customers anything of value not in the policy to induce a sale, and some are loosening this (NCOIL Rebate Reform Model, Nov 2025). **Free sign-up is fine.** Cash back, gift cards or sharing our commission are not, unless the state allows it. Check every promotion with counsel.
- **Advertising and marketing:** no misleading claims. "Save up to 70%" or "$400 less" must be substantiated by real quotes. Disclose that savings vary. **Never imply VA or government affiliation** (no seals, no "VA-approved").
- **Suitability and disclosure:** explain coverage trade-offs (the deductible comparison does this), disclose how we're paid, and show license numbers.
- **Unfair trade practices:** no twisting (switching policies to the customer's detriment), no misrepresenting coverage, no unfair discrimination.

## Money
- **Direct bill only** (the carrier collects the premium) means we never hold customer funds. If we ever collect premium, states require **fiduciary/trust accounting**, with separate accounts and records. Avoid that in Phase 1.

## Records and privacy
- **Record retention:** keep applications, quotes, advice, chats, emails and approvals for the state-required period (often 3–5+ years; use the longest across our states).
- **Privacy:** GLBA and state insurance privacy laws (NAIC Model 672 and 668 data security in many states): privacy notice, data security program, and breach notification.
- **Complaint log:** many states require a complaint register; respond to DOI complaint inquiries by their deadline.

## AI-specific compliance (our differentiator, so we must get it right)
Follow the NAIC AI Model Bulletin's principles in every state, even those that haven't adopted it:
- **Governance:** a written AI program owned by the DRLP, plus a model inventory.
- **Human in the loop:** a licensed advisor approves every bind, every coverage reduction and every claim-related recommendation.
- **Testing:** accuracy of the coverage explanations, and checks for unfair discrimination (for example, outcomes by age).
- **Audit trail:** log what the AI said, what data it used, and who approved it.
- **Transparency:** tell customers they're dealing with an AI agent backed by licensed humans.
- **Vendor oversight:** contracts with LLM and data vendors covering security and data use.

## Chat- and email-only support (decision 2026-11-22)
- **Upside for compliance:** every conversation is written, timestamped and attributable to a licensed person, which makes for strong E&O defense and easy audits.
- **Check per state:** whether a licensed agency must publish a **phone number** or physical address on its license record, ads or policy documents. Some forms and DOI records ask for one; a business line routed to voicemail and ticketing may satisfy this. **Verify with counsel.**
- **Emergencies:** after a loss, customers must reach the **carrier's 24/7 claims line**. Show it prominently in the app and in every claims message.
- **Accessibility:** offer large text, read-aloud and family-member access so customers who can't easily type still get help (ADA and WCAG 2.2 AA).
