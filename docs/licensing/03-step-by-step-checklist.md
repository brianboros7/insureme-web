# 03 — Step-by-step checklist: from zero to the first policy

Do these in order; each step depends on the one before. Owner: CEO unless noted.

## Step 0: decide (week 0)
- [ ] **Pick the DRLP:** a founder who will get licensed, or a licensed officer-level hire (Head of Advisors). Texas and Illinois effectively require an officer.
- [ ] **Pick the home state** for the company and the DRLP's resident license. It's simplest if both are a launch state (Texas or Virginia are good fits for veterans).
- [ ] **Trademark and name check** for "InsureMe" (USPTO plus state DOI name rules). Pick the legal and DBA names.
- [ ] **Engage an insurance regulatory attorney** (licensing, agency agreements, AI disclosures). Budgeted inside the $90K.

## Step 1: form the company (weeks 1–2)
- [ ] Form the LLC or C-corp (a C-corp is usually better for venture investors) in the home state, or in Delaware with a home-state foreign registration.
- [ ] Get an EIN and open a business bank account.
- [ ] Register as a foreign entity in each launch state where required. **Virginia:** register with the SCC within 90 days of the agency license.
- [ ] File the DBA if needed.

## Step 2: license the people (weeks 1–8, in parallel with step 1)
- [ ] DRLP takes P&C pre-licensing (if the home state requires it): about 40 hours in Illinois.
- [ ] Schedule and pass the state P&C exam.
- [ ] Fingerprints and background check where required.
- [ ] Apply for the **resident individual license** (state portal or NIPR).
- [ ] Apply for **non-resident individual licenses** in the other three launch states through NIPR.
- [ ] Repeat for each licensed advisor as we hire. Target 2–3 licensed advisors at launch.

## Step 3: license the agency (weeks 6–10)
- [ ] Apply for the **resident business entity license** in the home state with the DRLP named.
- [ ] Apply for **non-resident business entity licenses** in the other three states through NIPR (needs the home-state entity license first).
- [ ] Add each licensed advisor to the agency's roster where states require it.
- [ ] Get the agency's **National Producer Number (NPN)**.

## Step 4: protect the agency (weeks 6–10)
- [ ] Buy **agency E&O** ($1M/$1M is a common carrier minimum) with technology/AI-assisted advice and cyber coverage.
- [ ] Buy cyber insurance if E&O doesn't include it.
- [ ] Decide on **direct bill only** (the carrier collects premium), so we never hold customer money and avoid premium trust-account rules.

## Step 5: carriers (weeks 8–16+)
- [ ] Join an **agency network or aggregator** for day-one market access (see [04](04-carrier-appointments.md)).
- [ ] Apply for direct appointments with API-ready homeowners carriers in each state.
- [ ] Sign agency agreements, with an attorney reviewing commission, ownership of renewals ("book ownership") and AI/automation clauses.
- [ ] Confirm each carrier has filed our appointment with the state *before* we quote its products.

## Step 6: compliance setup before launch (weeks 10–16)
- [ ] Written **compliance manual**: advertising review, anti-rebating, complaints, records retention, privacy (GLBA), licensing renewals and CE tracking.
- [ ] **AI governance program** in the style of the NAIC bulletin: model inventory, testing for accuracy and unfair discrimination, human approval before binding or reducing coverage, audit logs.
- [ ] Customer disclosures: that they're talking to an AI agent, who the licensed agency is (license numbers in every state), how we're paid (commission), and "not affiliated with the VA."
- [ ] Chat and email ticket workflow: licensed advisors answer every human-requested thread; response time targets; escalation to the carrier's 24/7 claims line for emergencies.
- [ ] License numbers in the app footer, website and emails as each state requires.

## Step 7: launch
- [ ] Soft launch in the first licensed state with real quotes, then switch on the other three as appointments come through.
- [ ] Calendar every renewal (individual and entity, all four states) and CE deadline.
