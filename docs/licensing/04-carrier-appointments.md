# 04 — Carrier appointments: getting carriers to sell through us

A license lets us sell insurance; an **appointment** lets us sell a specific carrier's policies. Without appointments the AI has nothing to quote.

## The problem for a new agency
Carriers prefer agencies with proven volume, a loss history and experienced staff. A brand-new, AI-first agency with no book of business often gets told "come back when you have $X in premium." Homeowners carriers in hail and hurricane states (Texas especially) are also limiting new business.

## Three ways in (use all three)

| Route | How it works | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Agency network / aggregator** (for example SIAA, Smart Choice, Renaissance, Iroquois, Keystone; digital aggregators) | We join a network that already holds carrier contracts; we sell under its appointments | Day-one access to many carriers; no volume minimums | Revenue share (commonly 10–30% of commission); network rules; we may not own renewals; may limit API access |
| **2. Direct appointments with API-first carriers and MGAs** | Apply to carriers that sell through digital agencies and offer quote/bind APIs | Full commission; real-time quotes for the AI; we own the relationship | Slower; selective; may need volume commitments |
| **3. Comparative rater + standard carriers** | Use a comparative rater (for example EZLynx, PL Rating, Bold Penguin-style tools) with carriers we're appointed with | Many quotes from one integration | Raters need appointments too; per-seat cost |

**Recommended path:** start with a network for breadth (route 1), add 2–3 direct API carriers per state (route 2) as volume grows, and move high-volume carriers to direct contracts.

## What carriers will ask for
- Agency license(s) and NPN; DRLP and advisor licenses
- **E&O certificate** (often $1M/$1M minimum)
- Business plan, target market and expected premium (use doc 07)
- Background checks on principals
- How we market (veterans and 65+ affinity marketing gets scrutiny; no implied VA endorsement)
- **How the AI works**: human approval, accuracy, complaints, data security. Have a one-page AI governance summary ready.

## Contract terms to negotiate (attorney review)
- **Commission:** new and renewal rates (homeowners is commonly around 10–15%)
- **Book ownership:** who owns the customer at renewal if we leave the network or the carrier
- **Automation and AI clauses:** whether the carrier allows automated quoting and binding, and under what controls
- **Data use:** what we can store and reuse for re-shopping
- **Termination and run-off**
