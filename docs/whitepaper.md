# The Agentic Broker: Fairer Homeowners Insurance for Veterans and Elders

**InsureMe White Paper. Working draft v0.1, 2026-11-22.**
*This is a living document. Each research sprint adds to it. Detailed sources are in docs 01–13.*

---

## Abstract
US homeowners pay about $173B a year in premiums. Roughly a quarter of every premium dollar goes to distribution, administration and taxes rather than claims. Brokers earn about 12–14% of premium, yet most homeowners are never re-shopped, get little help at claim time, and are increasingly dropped or priced out. Veterans (about 77% homeownership) and adults 65+ (about 79% homeownership) are especially exposed: many live on fixed incomes, and older homeowners are prime targets for post-disaster scams. This paper argues that an **agentic-AI broker, supervised by licensed humans**, can do the broker's whole job continuously and at a fraction of the cost. It sets out how that model can lower what customers pay by about 20–40% typically, and by up to about 70% for overpriced, mitigation-eligible households.

## 1. How insurance works today
*(Summary of docs 01, 03, 04, 05.)*
- **Carriers** price and carry risk. Their **underwriters** decide whether to accept a risk, on what terms, and at what price, working from state-filed rates. Most homeowners policies are priced by algorithms.
- **Brokers and agents** shop, advise, bind and service. They are paid by commission built into the premium. Independent agents place about 50% of homeowners premium. Homeowners has historically resisted direct selling.
- **MGAs** underwrite on a carrier's behalf under delegated authority. **Fronting carriers** rent their licensed paper. This is how new insurers start.
- **Claims** run from first notice of loss through investigation, evaluation, settlement, payment and subrogation. In 2025, home claims averaged 44 days to final payment, the longest J.D. Power has recorded.

## 2. The problem
1. **Cost:** homeowners premiums rose about 13% in 2024. About $24 of every $100 pays for distribution and admin.
2. **Availability:** non-renewals outnumber new policies in 46 of 58 California counties. Florida's rates are 3–4 times the national average.
3. **Service gap:** small personal accounts aren't worth a human broker's time, so re-shopping and claim help rarely happen.
4. **Vulnerability:** older homeowners face scams, fixed incomes, and technology that isn't accessible.

## 3. The segment: veterans and elders
*(Detail in doc 08.)* About 13M veteran homeowners and a fast-growing 65+ population (65+ households up about 20% by 2035). The incumbents (USAA, AFI, AARP/The Hartford, VFW via Lockton Affinity) leave gaps in availability (no AARP program in CA or FL), advocacy and accessibility.

## 4. The thesis: an agentic broker
*(Full model in doc 13.)* Five stacked levers:
1. AI replaces broker labor: distribution and servicing cost falls about 63–70%, or about 18–22% of premium.
2. Continuous re-shopping removes the loyalty penalty.
3. Mitigation (for example a FORTIFIED roof: 20–55% off the wind premium in Alabama) lowers both price and actual losses.
4. Right-sized coverage.
5. Returning underwriting profit (MGA or reciprocal phase).

Stacked together, these give about 26% savings for already well-shopped customers, about 45% for typical customers, and about 71% for overpriced, wind-exposed, mitigated homes. **These are model estimates to be validated by a 200-household quote study.**

## 5. The InsureMe model
- **Phase 1:** licensed digital agency + AI broker + veteran-staffed human advisors. Revenue is commission.
- **Phase 2:** MGA with its own homeowners product on fronting paper. Lower expense loads and a mitigation-focused book turn the AI's efficiency into lower filed rates.
- **Phase 3:** a carrier or reciprocal exchange, if warranted.

## 6. Responsible AI and regulation
Producer licensing (NAIC Producer Licensing Model Act); the NAIC Model Bulletin on AI use; anti-rebating laws; substantiating advertised savings; elder-exploitation safeguards; no implied VA or government affiliation; WCAG accessibility; public-adjuster licensing limits on claims advocacy.
