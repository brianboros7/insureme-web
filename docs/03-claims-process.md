# 03 — The Claims Process, Start to Finish

The process is general P&C (home and auto). Sources disagree on the number of steps; this is the common sequence.

| # | Stage | What happens | Who's involved | Where it hurts |
|---|-------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| 0 | **Loss occurs** | Accident, fire, theft, storm | Policyholder | Panic. People don't know what to document. |
| 1 | **First Notice of Loss (FNOL)** | The policyholder reports the loss by phone, app, agent or web, giving policy number, date, description, photos and police report. | Policyholder, agent/broker, carrier call center | Repeating information; the agent is often just a go-between |
| 2 | **Assignment** | The carrier opens a claim file, gives it a number, sets an initial reserve and assigns an adjuster (staff or independent, desk or field). | Carrier claims dept | Waiting for the first contact |
| 3 | **Coverage verification** | Checks the policy was in force, the peril is covered, deductibles and limits, exclusions, and fraud flags. May send a reservation-of-rights letter. | Adjuster, coverage counsel | Policy wording customers never read |
| 4 | **Investigation** | Inspection (in person, virtual or drone), statements, recorded interviews, documents, liability decision in auto | Adjuster, inspectors, experts | Slowest step. Scheduling, follow-ups. |
| 5 | **Evaluation / estimate** | Repair or replacement cost, actual cash value vs replacement cost, depreciation, using CCC (auto) or Xactimate (property) | Adjuster, contractors, body shops | Disputes over the estimate amount |
| 6 | **Negotiation & settlement** | Offer, counter-offer, agreement. Disputes can go to appraisal, a public adjuster, a complaint to the state DOI, or a lawsuit. | Adjuster, policyholder, public adjuster, attorneys | Power gap between customer and carrier |
| 7 | **Payment** | Check or electronic payment, often naming the mortgage lender or lienholder as co-payee. Supplemental payments for hidden damage. | Carrier, lender | Mortgage co-payee checks get stuck |
| 8 | **Subrogation & salvage** | The carrier recovers from the at-fault party or their insurer and sells salvage. The deductible may be refunded. Can start on day 1 and take 10 days to a year. | Carrier recovery unit | Customer waits for the deductible refund |
| 9 | **Close** | File closed. Claim history goes to the CLUE database and affects renewal and pricing. | Carrier, LexisNexis CLUE | Surprise premium increase at renewal |

## The numbers
- **2025 home claims:** 44 days on average from first notice to final payment, and 32.4 days from filing to completed repairs. Both are the longest since J.D. Power began the study in 2008. ([J.D. Power 2025](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2025-us-property-claims-satisfaction-study))
- **2026 study:** slightly better, at 40.7 days to final payment and 29.6 days to repair. ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/03/17/862324.htm))
- Claims closed within **10 days** score 762/1000. Repairs that take more than 31 days score **595**.
- 87% of Gen Z and Millennial customers are comfortable handling the whole claim digitally. Mobile apps produce the highest satisfaction of any channel.
- Carriers meet customers' expectations for digital status updates only **22%** of the time ([J.D. Power Claims Digital Experience 2025](https://www.jdpower.com/sites/default/files/file/2025-11-26/2025160%20U.S.%20Claims%20Digital%20Experience%20Study%20.pdf)).

Process sources: [Big "I" adjuster's perspective](https://www.independentagent.com/vu_resource/the-claims-handling-process-for-agents-an-adjusters-perspective/), [Open Exam Prep, FNOL to settlement](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ains/claims-handling/claims-process), [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/article/insurance/T028-C000-S002-how-to-get-your-insurer-to-pay-your-home-and-auto-claims).

## What the broker does in a claim today
Helps file the first notice of loss, explains coverage, pushes the carrier when things stall, and sometimes advocates on disputed amounts. **This is the main thing customers say they'd miss without a broker**, so an app that cuts out the broker has to cover it with live claim tracking, guided documentation, and an escalation path.

## Regulation to know
Every state has Unfair Claims Settlement Practices rules (based on the NAIC model) that set deadlines to acknowledge a claim, accept or deny it, and pay. These deadlines are often 10–15 days to acknowledge and 30–40 days to decide. Exact deadlines vary by state and are still **to be verified**.
