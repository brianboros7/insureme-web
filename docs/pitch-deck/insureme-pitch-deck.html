The problem

Last spring, hail hit Jack’s roof. He paid $7,600 before his insurance paid a dollar.

Jack already pays about $4,100 a year. His policy has a 2% wind and hail deductible on his $380,000 home, normal in Texas, where the average is 2.2% ($7,761). In Indiana, his mom Linda faces rising premiums alone.

Business model

Agency first, then our own product

Phase 1

Licensed digital agency

Carrier commission of about 10–15%. About $264 a year per policy, recurring at renewal. Bundles: auto, umbrella, flood.

Phase 2

MGA on fronting paper

Our own homeowners product for the segment. Lower expense load becomes a lower filed price. Commission + fees + profit share.

Phase 3

Carrier or reciprocal

Only if Phase 2 proves the loss ratio. Full premium and underwriting profit returned to members.

$264 = $2,200 premium × 12% commission (assumption to validate per state).

Phase 1 is capital-light and fast. The AI’s savings only become a lower price once we price the product ourselves in Phase 2. Until then, savings come from shopping, mitigation and right-sizing.

Regulation as a moat

Licensed, compliant and at the table

Licensed agency

A producer license in each state, with a designated licensed producer accountable. The AI acts for the agency, never instead of it.

Responsible AI

Built to the NAIC AI Model Bulletin, adopted by about 25 jurisdictions including Illinois and Virginia. Human approval on binding.

Policy agenda

Support rebating reform (the NCOIL 2025 model), mitigation discounts, and elder and veteran scam protections.

50-state playbook

Rate regimes, rebating, residual markets and lobbying mapped state by state. Expect agent associations to push back.

Insurance is regulated state by state under McCarran-Ferguson. We treat compliance as a moat: licensed humans in the loop, audit trails, and early engagement with regulators and legislators. We never imply VA or government affiliation.
